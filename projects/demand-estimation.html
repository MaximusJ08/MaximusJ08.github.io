Estimating hidden demand when fashion items sell out.

Evaluated an EM-MNL demand estimator under stockout censoring in fashion retail, using simulations and multi-retailer sales data. The dissertation also introduces a neurosymbolic extension that uses style-based substitution rules as soft constraints.

The problem

When a product is out of stock, recorded sales understate its true demand. Some customers switch to another item, while others leave without buying, so sales data can make both the unavailable product and its substitutes look different from what customers actually wanted.

I tested how reliably the EM-MNL estimator can recover that hidden demand. The study first established a simulation-based baseline, then examined when its assumptions break down and compared the results with sales data from multiple fashion retailers.

What the study found

  • Under ideal simulated conditions, EM-MNL recovered demand more accurately than naive baselines, Poisson regression, and neural networks.
  • Robustness thresholds for substitution correlation and arrival over-dispersion were checked across 12 retail stores.
  • Style-based soft constraints improved recovery when data were sparse if the rules were correct; incorrect rules could reduce performance.

Neurosymbolic extension

The extension adds product-style knowledge to the estimator as a soft constraint, guiding it toward plausible substitutions without making those rules absolute. That makes the rule's quality visible: useful style knowledge can help with sparse sales data, while a poor rule can be harmful. Disabling the rule returns the estimator to its original form.
